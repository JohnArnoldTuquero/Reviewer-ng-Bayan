What is Percent?

A percent means "per hundred." The word percent comes from the Latin phrase per centum, which means "by the hundred." The symbol for percent is %.

A percent tells us how many parts there are out of 100 equal parts. For example, 25% means 25 out of 100.

Examples

50% means 50 out of 100.

75% means 75 out of 100.

10% means 10 out of 100.

Remember: Percent = Parts out of 100

Percent as a Fraction

Since percent means "out of 100," a percent can easily be written as a fraction with 100 as the denominator.

To convert a percent to a fraction, write the percent number over 100 and simplify if possible.

Examples

25% = 25/100 = 1/4

50% = 50/100 = 1/2

75% = 75/100 = 3/4

20% = 20/100 = 1/5

Rule

Percent → Fraction: Put the number over 100, then simplify.

Percent as a Decimal

To convert a percent to a decimal, divide the percent by 100. This is also the same as moving the decimal point two places to the left.

Examples

25% = 0.25

50% = 0.50 = 0.5

75% = 0.75

8% = 0.08

125% = 1.25

Rule

Percent → Decimal: Move the decimal point two places to the left.

Converting Fractions to Percent

To convert a fraction to a percent, first convert the fraction into a decimal, then multiply by 100.

Example 1

Convert 1/2 to percent.

1 ÷ 2 = 0.5

0.5 × 100 = 50

Therefore: 1/2 = 50%

Example 2

Convert 3/4 to percent.

3 ÷ 4 = 0.75

0.75 × 100 = 75

Therefore: 3/4 = 75%

Shortcut

If the denominator can easily be changed to 100, you can use that method directly.

3/4 = 75/100 = 75%

Converting Decimals to Percent

To convert a decimal to a percent, multiply the decimal by 100 or move the decimal point two places to the right.

Examples

0.25 × 100 = 25%

0.5 × 100 = 50%

0.75 × 100 = 75%

0.08 × 100 = 8%

1.25 × 100 = 125%

Rule

Decimal → Percent: Move the decimal point two places to the right and add the percent sign.

Finding the Percent of a Number

To find a percent of a number, convert the percent to a decimal and multiply it by the number.

Formula

Part = Percent × Whole

Remember that the percent must be written as a decimal when using this formula.

Example 1

Find 20% of 150.

20% = 0.20

0.20 × 150 = 30

Therefore, 20% of 150 is 30.

Example 2

Find 15% of 200.

15% = 0.15

0.15 × 200 = 30

Therefore, 15% of 200 is 30.

Finding What Percent One Number Is of Another

Sometimes we know the part and the whole, but we need to find the percentage.

Formula

Percent = (Part ÷ Whole) × 100

Example

A student answered 45 questions correctly out of 50. What percent of the questions were answered correctly?

Percent = (45 ÷ 50) × 100

Percent = 0.9 × 100

Percent = 90%

Therefore, the student answered 90% of the questions correctly.

Finding the Whole Number

Sometimes we know the percentage and the part, but we need to find the original or whole amount.

Formula

Whole = Part ÷ Percent

The percent must be written as a decimal before dividing.

Example

30 is 20% of what number?

20% = 0.20

Whole = 30 ÷ 0.20

Whole = 150

Therefore, 30 is 20% of 150.

Percent Increase

A percent increase tells us how much a quantity has increased compared with its original value.

Formula

Percent Increase = ((New Value − Original Value) ÷ Original Value) × 100

Example

A price increased from ₱200 to ₱250. What is the percent increase?

Increase = ₱250 − ₱200 = ₱50

Percent Increase = (50 ÷ 200) × 100

Percent Increase = 25%

Therefore, the price increased by 25%.

Percent Decrease

A percent decrease tells us how much a quantity has decreased compared with its original value.

Formula

Percent Decrease = ((Original Value − New Value) ÷ Original Value) × 100

Example

A price decreased from ₱500 to ₱400. What is the percent decrease?

Decrease = ₱500 − ₱400 = ₱100

Percent Decrease = (100 ÷ 500) × 100

Percent Decrease = 20%

Therefore, the price decreased by 20%.

Discounts

A discount is an amount deducted from the original price of an item.

Formula for Discount

Discount = Original Price × Discount Rate

Formula for Sale Price

Sale Price = Original Price − Discount

Example

A shirt costs ₱800 and is on sale for 25% off. Find the discount and the sale price.

25% = 0.25

Discount = ₱800 × 0.25

Discount = ₱200

Sale Price = ₱800 − ₱200

Sale Price = ₱600

Therefore, the discount is ₱200 and the sale price is ₱600.

Sales Tax

A sales tax is an additional amount added to the original price of a product or service.

Formula for Tax

Tax = Original Price × Tax Rate

Formula for Total Price

Total Price = Original Price + Tax

Example

Suppose an item costs ₱1,000 and has a 10% tax.

10% = 0.10

Tax = ₱1,000 × 0.10

Tax = ₱100

Total Price = ₱1,000 + ₱100

Total Price = ₱1,100

Therefore, the total price is ₱1,100.

Profit and Loss

In buying and selling, profit happens when the selling price is greater than the cost price. A loss happens when the selling price is less than the cost price.

Profit

Profit = Selling Price − Cost Price

Loss

Loss = Cost Price − Selling Price

Profit Percent

Profit Percent = (Profit ÷ Cost Price) × 100

Loss Percent

Loss Percent = (Loss ÷ Cost Price) × 100

Example: Profit

A product costs ₱500 and is sold for ₱600.

Profit = ₱600 − ₱500 = ₱100

Profit Percent = (₱100 ÷ ₱500) × 100

Profit Percent = 20%

Therefore, the profit is ₱100, or 20% of the cost price.

Example: Loss

A product costs ₱500 but is sold for ₱450.

Loss = ₱500 − ₱450 = ₱50

Loss Percent = (₱50 ÷ ₱500) × 100

Loss Percent = 10%

Therefore, the loss is ₱50, or 10% of the cost price.

Summary

Percent means per hundred and is represented by the symbol %.

Important Conversions

Percent → Fraction: Put the percent over 100 and simplify.

Percent → Decimal: Divide by 100 or move the decimal two places to the left.

Fraction → Percent: Divide the numerator by the denominator, then multiply by 100.

Decimal → Percent: Multiply by 100 or move the decimal two places to the right.

Important Formulas

Part = Percent × Whole

Percent = (Part ÷ Whole) × 100

Whole = Part ÷ Percent

Percent Increase = ((New − Original) ÷ Original) × 100

Percent Decrease = ((Original − New) ÷ Original) × 100

Discount = Original Price × Discount Rate

Sale Price = Original Price − Discount

Tax = Original Price × Tax Rate

Total Price = Original Price + Tax

Understanding percentages is important in solving problems involving discounts, taxes, increases, decreases, profit, loss, grades, and many real-life situations.
