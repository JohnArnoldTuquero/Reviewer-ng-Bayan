What is a Polynomial?

A polynomial is an algebraic expression made up of one or more terms. The terms are separated by addition or subtraction signs, and each term can contain constants, variables, and non-negative whole-number exponents.

Examples of polynomials include:

3x + 5

x2 + 4x - 7

2x3 - 5x2 + x + 9

Expressions such as x2 + 3x + 2 are polynomials because the variable has whole-number exponents.

Expressions containing variables with negative or fractional exponents, such as x-1 or x1/2, are not polynomials.

Parts of a Polynomial

A polynomial is made up of several important parts. Consider the polynomial:

4x3 - 7x2 + 5x - 9

The numbers and variables have different roles.

Variable

The variable is the letter representing an unknown or changing value. In the example, x is the variable.

Coefficient

The coefficient is the numerical factor multiplying a variable.

In 4x3, the coefficient is 4.

In -7x2, the coefficient is -7.

Constant

A constant is a number without a variable. In the polynomial above, -9 is the constant.

Exponent

The exponent tells how many times the variable is used as a factor.

In x3, the exponent is 3.

Terms of a Polynomial

A term is a number, variable, or product of numbers and variables separated from other terms by addition or subtraction.

Consider:

6x3 - 4x2 + 8x - 10

The terms are:

6x3

-4x2

8x

-10

This polynomial has 4 terms.

Terms that have the same variables raised to the same powers are called like terms. For example, 3x2 and 8x2 are like terms.

However, 3x2 and 3x are unlike terms because their exponents are different.

Degree of a Polynomial

The degree of a polynomial is the greatest exponent of the variable with a non-zero coefficient.

For example:

5x4 + 2x3 - 7x + 1

The highest exponent is 4, so the polynomial has degree 4.

Examples

3x + 5 → Degree 1

x2 - 4x + 3 → Degree 2

2x3 + x2 - 8 → Degree 3

7x5 - 2x2 + x - 1 → Degree 5

A non-zero constant such as 8 has degree 0.

Types of Polynomials

Polynomials can be classified according to the number of terms they contain and their degree.

Based on Number of Terms

Monomial – one term.

Binomial – two terms.

Trinomial – three terms.

Polynomials with four or more terms are usually described by their number of terms, such as a four-term polynomial.

Based on Degree

A polynomial can also be called:

Constant – degree 0.

Linear – degree 1.

Quadratic – degree 2.

Cubic – degree 3.

Quartic – degree 4.

Quintic – degree 5.

Monomials

A monomial is a polynomial with exactly one term.

Examples include:

5x

7x2

-3x4

12

A monomial can contain a constant, a variable, or a product of constants and variables.

For example, 4x3y2 is a monomial because it contains only one term.

Binomials

A binomial is a polynomial containing exactly two terms.

Examples:

x + 5

3x2 - 7

4x3 + 2x

When simplifying a binomial, combine like terms only when they have the same variable parts and exponents.

Trinomials

A trinomial is a polynomial containing exactly three terms.

Examples:

x2 + 5x + 6

2x3 - 4x + 1

5x2 + 3x - 8

A common quadratic trinomial has the form:

ax2 + bx + c

where a, b, and c are constants and a ≠ 0.

Standard Form of a Polynomial

The standard form of a polynomial is written with the terms arranged from the highest exponent to the lowest exponent.

For example:

3x + 7x3 - 2 + x2

is not in standard form.

Arrange the terms according to descending powers of x:

7x3 + x2 + 3x - 2

This is the standard form.

Writing polynomials in standard form makes it easier to identify their degree and perform operations with them.

Evaluating Polynomials

To evaluate a polynomial, substitute the given value of the variable and simplify.

Example:

Evaluate:

2x2 + 3x - 5

when x = 2.

Substitute 2 for x:

2(2)2 + 3(2) - 5

Apply the exponent first:

2(4) + 6 - 5

Multiply:

8 + 6 - 5

Therefore:

9

So, when x = 2, the value of the polynomial is 9.

Adding Polynomials

To add polynomials, combine like terms.

Example:

(3x2 + 5x + 2) + (2x2 - 3x + 4)

Group like terms:

3x2 + 2x2 + 5x - 3x + 2 + 4

Combine them:

5x2 + 2x + 6

Therefore:

(3x2 + 5x + 2) + (2x2 - 3x + 4) = 5x2 + 2x + 6

Subtracting Polynomials

When subtracting polynomials, distribute the negative sign to every term in the polynomial being subtracted, then combine like terms.

Example:

(5x2 + 4x - 3) - (2x2 - x + 5)

Distribute the negative sign:

5x2 + 4x - 3 - 2x2 + x - 5

Combine like terms:

3x2 + 5x - 8

Therefore:

(5x2 + 4x - 3) - (2x2 - x + 5) = 3x2 + 5x - 8

Multiplying Polynomials

To multiply polynomials, multiply each term of one polynomial by each term of the other polynomial and then combine like terms.

Example:

(x + 3)(x + 2)

Multiply each term:

x(x) + x(2) + 3(x) + 3(2)

Simplify:

x2 + 2x + 3x + 6

Combine like terms:

x2 + 5x + 6

Therefore:

(x + 3)(x + 2) = x2 + 5x + 6

Distributive Property

The distributive property states that a number or term multiplied by a sum or difference can be distributed to every term inside the parentheses.

The general form is:

a(b + c) = ab + ac

For example:

3(x + 4)

Distribute 3:

3x + 12

For a polynomial:

2x(x2 + 3x - 5)

Multiply 2x by every term:

2x3 + 6x2 - 10x

The distributive property is one of the main methods used when multiplying polynomials.

Special Products

Some polynomial products follow useful patterns called special products.

Square of a Binomial

The formula is:

(a + b)2 = a2 + 2ab + b2

Example:

(x + 3)2

Using the formula:

x2 + 2(x)(3) + 32

= x2 + 6x + 9

Difference of Two Squares

The formula is:

(a + b)(a - b) = a2 - b2

Example:

(x + 5)(x - 5)

= x2 - 25

Recognizing these patterns can make polynomial multiplication faster and easier.

Polynomial Word Problems

Polynomials can be used to represent quantities in real-world situations involving measurements, areas, costs, and other changing values.

Example: Area of a Rectangle

Suppose a rectangle has a length of x + 5 meters and a width of x + 2 meters.

The area of a rectangle is:

Area = length × width

Substitute the expressions:

Area = (x + 5)(x + 2)

Multiply:

Area = x2 + 2x + 5x + 10

Combine like terms:

Area = x2 + 7x + 10

Therefore, the area can be represented by the polynomial:

x2 + 7x + 10 square meters.

This shows how polynomial expressions can represent measurements and quantities in practical problems.

Summary

A polynomial is an algebraic expression made up of one or more terms with variables raised to non-negative whole-number exponents.

Important concepts to remember:

• Term – a part of a polynomial separated by addition or subtraction.

• Coefficient – the numerical factor of a variable.

• Constant – a number without a variable.

• Degree – the highest exponent of the variable.

• Monomial – a polynomial with one term.

• Binomial – a polynomial with two terms.

• Trinomial – a polynomial with three terms.

When working with polynomials:

1. Identify the terms and their coefficients.

2. Arrange the polynomial in standard form when needed.

3. Combine like terms when adding or subtracting.

4. Use the distributive property when multiplying.

5. Apply special product formulas when appropriate.

Understanding polynomials is an important foundation for solving equations, factoring expressions, and studying more advanced algebra.
