What is a Rate?

A rate is a comparison of two quantities that have different units. It tells us how much of one quantity corresponds to a certain amount of another quantity.

Rates are commonly used in everyday situations involving distance, time, money, quantity, speed, and other measurements.

Examples

A car travels 120 kilometers in 2 hours.

The rate is:

120 kilometers per 2 hours

A worker earns ₱500 for 8 hours of work.

The rate is:

₱500 per 8 hours

A machine produces 300 items in 5 minutes.

The rate is:

300 items per 5 minutes

Writing Rates

A rate can be written using the word "per", a colon :, or as a fraction.

Example

A bus travels 180 kilometers in 3 hours.

The rate can be written as:

180 kilometers per 3 hours

or:

180 km : 3 h

or:

180 km / 3 h

These all represent the same rate.

Important

Always pay attention to the order of the quantities. For example, kilometers per hour is different from hours per kilometer.

What is a Unit Rate?

A unit rate is a rate in which the second quantity is exactly 1 unit.

The word "unit" means one.

For example:

120 kilometers in 2 hours is a rate.

60 kilometers per 1 hour is a unit rate.

Therefore:

120 km / 2 h = 60 km / 1 h

Examples of Unit Rates

₱50 per 1 kilogram

60 kilometers per 1 hour

5 pages per 1 minute

8 items per 1 box

Finding a Unit Rate

To find a unit rate, divide the first quantity by the second quantity so that the second quantity becomes 1.

Formula

Unit Rate = First Quantity ÷ Second Quantity

Example 1

A car travels 240 kilometers in 4 hours. Find its unit rate in kilometers per hour.

Unit rate = 240 ÷ 4

Unit rate = 60

Therefore, the car travels at 60 km/h.

Example 2

Five notebooks cost ₱150. Find the cost per notebook.

Unit price = ₱150 ÷ 5

Unit price = ₱30

Therefore, each notebook costs ₱30.

Rates as Fractions

A rate can be represented as a fraction. The numerator represents one quantity, while the denominator represents the other quantity.

Example

A runner travels 20 kilometers in 2 hours.

The rate can be written as:

20 km / 2 h

Divide:

20 ÷ 2 = 10

Therefore:

20 km / 2 h = 10 km / 1 h

The unit rate is 10 km/h.

Important

When dividing a rate, keep the units attached to the numbers. This helps you identify what the answer represents.

Equivalent Rates

Equivalent rates describe the same relationship even though they may use different quantities.

Example

A vehicle travels 100 kilometers in 2 hours.

Its rate is:

100 km / 2 h

Divide both quantities by 2:

100 ÷ 2 = 50

2 ÷ 2 = 1

Therefore:

100 km / 2 h = 50 km / 1 h

Both are equivalent rates.

Another Example

6 apples cost ₱120.

Divide both quantities by 6:

6 ÷ 6 = 1

₱120 ÷ 6 = ₱20

Therefore:

6 apples for ₱120 = 1 apple for ₱20

Comparing Rates

Unit rates make it easier to compare two or more rates. Convert each rate to the same unit rate first, then compare the results.

Example

Store A sells 5 kilograms of rice for ₱300.

Store B sells 4 kilograms of rice for ₱260.

Find the unit price of Store A:

₱300 ÷ 5 = ₱60 per kg

Find the unit price of Store B:

₱260 ÷ 4 = ₱65 per kg

Therefore, Store A has a unit price of ₱60 per kilogram, while Store B has a unit price of ₱65 per kilogram.

Important

When comparing rates, make sure the units are the same before deciding which rate is greater or smaller.

Rates and Proportions

Rates can be used with proportions to solve problems involving equivalent relationships.

If two rates are equivalent, they can be written as a proportion.

Example

If 3 notebooks cost ₱90, how much will 8 notebooks cost?

Set up a proportion:

3/90 = 8/x

Cross multiply:

3x = 90 × 8

3x = 720

x = 240

Therefore, 8 notebooks cost ₱240.

Checking Using Unit Rate

₱90 ÷ 3 = ₱30 per notebook.

8 × ₱30 = ₱240.

The answer is confirmed.

Speed as a Rate

Speed is a rate that compares distance traveled with the amount of time used.

Formula

Speed = Distance ÷ Time

Common units of speed include:

kilometers per hour (km/h)

meters per second (m/s)

miles per hour (mph)

Example

A car travels 180 kilometers in 3 hours. What is its average speed?

Speed = 180 ÷ 3

Speed = 60

Therefore, the average speed is 60 km/h.

Finding Distance

If speed and time are known:

Distance = Speed × Time

For example, a vehicle traveling at 50 km/h for 4 hours travels:

50 × 4 = 200 km

Finding Time

If distance and speed are known:

Time = Distance ÷ Speed

For example, a vehicle travels 240 km at 60 km/h:

240 ÷ 60 = 4 hours

Unit Price

A unit price tells us the cost of one unit of a product. It is a unit rate used when comparing prices.

Formula

Unit Price = Total Cost ÷ Number of Units

Example 1

A pack of 12 bottles costs ₱180. What is the price per bottle?

₱180 ÷ 12 = ₱15

Therefore, the unit price is ₱15 per bottle.

Example 2

A 2-kilogram bag of rice costs ₱120. What is the price per kilogram?

₱120 ÷ 2 = ₱60

Therefore, the unit price is ₱60 per kilogram.

Why Unit Price is Useful

Unit price allows us to compare products that come in different quantities or package sizes.

Rate and Unit Rate Word Problems

Rates and unit rates appear frequently in real-life problems involving shopping, travel, work, production, and measurements.

Example 1: Shopping

A box contains 10 pencils and costs ₱80. What is the cost per pencil?

Unit price = ₱80 ÷ 10

Unit price = ₱8

Therefore, each pencil costs ₱8.

Example 2: Travel

A motorcycle travels 150 kilometers in 3 hours. What is its average speed?

Speed = Distance ÷ Time

Speed = 150 ÷ 3

Speed = 50 km/h

Therefore, the motorcycle's average speed is 50 km/h.

Example 3: Work

A worker earns ₱2,400 for 6 days of work. How much does the worker earn per day?

Daily rate = ₱2,400 ÷ 6

Daily rate = ₱400

Therefore, the worker earns ₱400 per day.

Example 4: Production

A machine produces 500 bottles in 10 minutes. How many bottles does it produce per minute?

Unit rate = 500 ÷ 10

Unit rate = 50

Therefore, the machine produces 50 bottles per minute.

Summary

A rate compares two quantities with different units.

A unit rate is a rate in which the second quantity is equal to 1.

Important Formulas

Unit Rate = First Quantity ÷ Second Quantity

Speed = Distance ÷ Time

Distance = Speed × Time

Time = Distance ÷ Speed

Unit Price = Total Cost ÷ Number of Units

Important Tips

Always identify the units being compared.

When finding a unit rate, divide until the second quantity becomes 1.

When comparing rates, convert them to the same units or unit rates first.

Rates can be written using the word "per," a colon, or a fraction.

For example:

60 kilometers per hour = 60 km/h = 60 km : 1 h

Rates and unit rates are useful in everyday situations such as shopping, calculating speed, comparing prices, determining wages, measuring production, and solving problems involving proportions.
