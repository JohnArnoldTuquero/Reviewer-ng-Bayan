Divisibility

Divisibility means that one number can be divided by another number without leaving a remainder.

For example:

12 ÷ 3 = 4

Since the answer is a whole number and there is no remainder, 12 is divisible by 3.

However:

13 ÷ 3 = 4 remainder 1

Therefore, 13 is not divisible by 3.

Divisibility Rules

Divisibility rules are shortcuts that allow us to determine whether a number is divisible by another number without performing the complete division.

These rules are especially useful when working with large numbers.

Divisibility Rule for 2

A number is divisible by 2 if its last digit is 0, 2, 4, 6, or 8.

Examples

24 is divisible by 2 because its last digit is 4.

136 is divisible by 2 because its last digit is 6.

500 is divisible by 2 because its last digit is 0.

Not Divisible

35 is not divisible by 2 because its last digit is 5.

127 is not divisible by 2 because its last digit is 7.

Shortcut

Check only the last digit.

Divisibility Rule for 3

A number is divisible by 3 if the sum of its digits is divisible by 3.

Example: 123

Add the digits:

1 + 2 + 3 = 6

Since 6 is divisible by 3, 123 is divisible by 3.

Example: 245

2 + 4 + 5 = 11

Since 11 is not divisible by 3, 245 is not divisible by 3.

Shortcut

Add all the digits. If the sum is divisible by 3, the original number is also divisible by 3.

Divisibility Rule for 4

A number is divisible by 4 if its last two digits form a number that is divisible by 4.

Example: 316

Look at the last two digits:

16

Since 16 is divisible by 4:

316 is divisible by 4.

Example: 528

The last two digits are:

28

Since 28 is divisible by 4, 528 is divisible by 4.

Example: 326

The last two digits are:

26

Since 26 is not divisible by 4, 326 is not divisible by 4.

Divisibility Rule for 5

A number is divisible by 5 if its last digit is 0 or 5.

Examples

25 is divisible by 5 because it ends in 5.

100 is divisible by 5 because it ends in 0.

735 is divisible by 5 because it ends in 5.

Not Divisible

42 is not divisible by 5 because its last digit is 2.

118 is not divisible by 5 because its last digit is 8.

Shortcut

Check only the last digit. It must be 0 or 5.

Divisibility Rule for 6

A number is divisible by 6 if it is divisible by both 2 and 3.

Example: 132

First, check divisibility by 2.

The last digit is 2, so 132 is divisible by 2.

Next, check divisibility by 3:

1 + 3 + 2 = 6

Since 6 is divisible by 3, 132 is divisible by 3.

Therefore:

132 is divisible by 6.

Important

To be divisible by 6, a number must satisfy both the divisibility rule for 2 and the divisibility rule for 3.

Divisibility Rule for 8

A number is divisible by 8 if its last three digits form a number that is divisible by 8.

Example: 1,024

Look at the last three digits:

024 = 24

Since 24 is divisible by 8:

1,024 is divisible by 8.

Example: 2,136

Look at the last three digits:

136

Since:

136 ÷ 8 = 17

2,136 is divisible by 8.

Shortcut

Check only the last three digits.

Divisibility Rule for 9

A number is divisible by 9 if the sum of its digits is divisible by 9.

Example: 729

Add the digits:

7 + 2 + 9 = 18

Since 18 is divisible by 9:

729 is divisible by 9.

Example: 542

5 + 4 + 2 = 11

Since 11 is not divisible by 9:

542 is not divisible by 9.

Shortcut

Add all the digits. If the sum is divisible by 9, the original number is divisible by 9.

Divisibility Rule for 10

A number is divisible by 10 if its last digit is 0.

Examples

50 is divisible by 10.

120 is divisible by 10.

4,000 is divisible by 10.

Not Divisible

125 is not divisible by 10 because it does not end in 0.

342 is not divisible by 10 because it does not end in 0.

Shortcut

Check only the last digit. It must be 0.

Divisibility Rule for 11

A number is divisible by 11 if the difference between the sums of alternating digits is either 0 or a multiple of 11.

Example: 121

Add the alternating digits:

1 + 1 = 2

The remaining digit is:

2

Find the difference:

2 - 2 = 0

Since the difference is 0:

121 is divisible by 11.

Example: 1,452

Add alternating digits:

1 + 5 = 6

Add the other digits:

4 + 2 = 6

Find the difference:

6 - 6 = 0

Therefore, 1,452 is divisible by 11.

Divisibility Rule for 12

A number is divisible by 12 if it is divisible by both 3 and 4.

Example: 348

First, check divisibility by 3:

3 + 4 + 8 = 15

Since 15 is divisible by 3, 348 is divisible by 3.

Next, check divisibility by 4.

The last two digits are:

48

Since 48 is divisible by 4, 348 is divisible by 4.

Therefore:

348 is divisible by 12.

Important

To be divisible by 12, a number must satisfy both the rules for 3 and 4.

Summary of Divisibility Rules

Divisible by 2: The last digit is 0, 2, 4, 6, or 8.

Divisible by 3: The sum of the digits is divisible by 3.

Divisible by 4: The last two digits form a number divisible by 4.

Divisible by 5: The last digit is 0 or 5.

Divisible by 6: The number is divisible by both 2 and 3.

Divisible by 8: The last three digits form a number divisible by 8.

Divisible by 9: The sum of the digits is divisible by 9.

Divisible by 10: The last digit is 0.

Divisible by 11: The difference between the sums of alternating digits is 0 or a multiple of 11.

Divisible by 12: The number is divisible by both 3 and 4.

Quick Review

Divisibility rules are shortcuts that help determine whether a number can be divided evenly without performing the complete division.

Remember to check the appropriate digits or digit sum depending on the divisor.
