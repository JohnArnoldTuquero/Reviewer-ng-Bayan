What is a Ratio?

A ratio is a comparison of two or more quantities using division. It tells us how much of one quantity there is compared with another quantity.

Ratios are commonly written using a colon :, as a fraction, or using the word "to."

Example

Suppose there are 3 red balls and 5 blue balls.

The ratio of red balls to blue balls is:

3 : 5

This can also be written as:

3 to 5

or

3/5

Therefore, a ratio compares quantities in a specific order.

Writing Ratios

When writing a ratio, it is important to identify the two quantities being compared and keep them in the correct order.

Example 1

A class has 12 boys and 18 girls. What is the ratio of boys to girls?

Boys = 12

Girls = 18

Ratio of boys to girls:

12 : 18

Example 2

Using the same class, what is the ratio of girls to boys?

18 : 12

Notice that changing the order changes the ratio.

Important

The phrase "A to B" means:

A : B

Simplifying Ratios

A ratio can be simplified just like a fraction. Divide both terms by their greatest common factor (GCF).

Example

Simplify the ratio 12 : 18.

The GCF of 12 and 18 is 6.

Divide both terms by 6:

12 ÷ 6 = 2

18 ÷ 6 = 3

Therefore:

12 : 18 = 2 : 3

Another Example

Simplify 20 : 30.

GCF = 10

20 ÷ 10 = 2

30 ÷ 10 = 3

Therefore:

20 : 30 = 2 : 3

Equivalent Ratios

Equivalent ratios are ratios that have the same value even though their numbers are different.

We can create equivalent ratios by multiplying or dividing both terms by the same nonzero number.

Example

Start with:

2 : 3

Multiply both terms by 2:

2 × 2 : 3 × 2 = 4 : 6

Multiply both terms by 3:

2 × 3 : 3 × 3 = 6 : 9

Therefore:

2 : 3 = 4 : 6 = 6 : 9

Important Rule

Whatever operation is done to one term of a ratio must also be done to the other term.

Ratios of Part to Whole

A ratio can compare one part of a group to the entire group. To find the whole, add all the parts together.

Example

A basket contains 4 apples and 6 oranges. What is the ratio of apples to the total number of fruits?

Total fruits:

4 + 6 = 10

Apples to total fruits:

4 : 10

Simplify:

2 : 5

Therefore, the ratio of apples to the total number of fruits is 2 : 5.

Comparing Ratios

Ratios can be compared by simplifying them or converting them into fractions or decimals.

Example

Compare 2 : 3 and 4 : 5.

Convert them into fractions:

2 : 3 = 2/3

4 : 5 = 4/5

Convert to decimals:

2 ÷ 3 ≈ 0.667

4 ÷ 5 = 0.8

Therefore:

4 : 5 is greater than 2 : 3.

Tip

Simplifying ratios first can make comparisons easier.

What is a Proportion?

A proportion is an equation that states that two ratios are equal.

For example:

2/3 = 4/6

These two ratios are equivalent, so they form a proportion.

Examples

3/5 = 6/10

4/7 = 8/14

5/8 = 15/24

Basic Form

A proportion can be written as:

a/b = c/d

where b and d are not zero.

Cross Multiplication

Cross multiplication is a method used to solve proportions.

If:

a/b = c/d

then:

a × d = b × c

Example

Determine whether the following forms a proportion:

2/3 = 4/6

Cross multiply:

2 × 6 = 12

3 × 4 = 12

Since both products are equal, the ratios form a proportion.

Important

When checking a proportion, the cross products must be equal.

Finding a Missing Value

Proportions can be used to find an unknown value.

Example 1

Find x:

3/5 = x/20

Cross multiply:

3 × 20 = 5 × x

60 = 5x

Divide both sides by 5:

x = 12

Therefore, x = 12.

Example 2

Find x:

4/7 = 20/x

Cross multiply:

4x = 7 × 20

4x = 140

x = 35

Therefore, x = 35.

Direct Proportion

Two quantities are in direct proportion when they increase or decrease together at a constant rate.

If one quantity doubles, the other quantity also doubles. If one quantity is reduced to half, the other quantity is also reduced to half.

Example

Suppose 2 notebooks cost ₱40. How much will 5 notebooks cost if the price per notebook remains the same?

Set up a proportion:

2/40 = 5/x

Cross multiply:

2x = 40 × 5

2x = 200

x = 100

Therefore, 5 notebooks cost ₱100.

Ratio and Proportion Word Problems

Ratio and proportion are often used in real-life situations involving money, quantities, measurements, mixtures, maps, recipes, and distances.

Example 1: Students

The ratio of boys to girls in a class is 2 : 3. If there are 10 boys, how many girls are there?

Set up the proportion:

2/3 = 10/x

Cross multiply:

2x = 30

x = 15

Therefore, there are 15 girls.

Example 2: Money

If 3 pens cost ₱45, how much will 8 pens cost at the same price per pen?

Set up the proportion:

3/45 = 8/x

Cross multiply:

3x = 360

x = 120

Therefore, 8 pens cost ₱120.

Ratios and Scale

A scale is a ratio used to represent a real object, distance, or measurement in a smaller or larger form.

Maps, drawings, models, and blueprints commonly use scale.

Example

Suppose a map has a scale of:

1 cm : 5 km

This means that every 1 centimeter on the map represents 5 kilometers in real life.

If two cities are 4 cm apart on the map:

4 × 5 = 20

Therefore, the actual distance is 20 km.

Using a Proportion

The same problem can be written as:

1/5 = 4/x

Cross multiply:

x = 20

Therefore, the actual distance is 20 km.

Summary

A ratio compares two or more quantities.

Ratios can be written using a colon, a fraction, or the word "to."

Example: 2 : 3 = 2/3 = 2 to 3

Important Ratio Rules

To simplify a ratio, divide both terms by their GCF.

To create an equivalent ratio, multiply or divide both terms by the same nonzero number.

Important Proportion Rule

A proportion states that two ratios are equal.

a/b = c/d

Cross multiplication gives:

a × d = b × c

Key Formulas

Ratio = Comparison of quantities

Equivalent ratios = Multiply or divide both terms by the same number

a/b = c/d → a × d = b × c

Ratios and proportions are useful for solving problems involving quantities, prices, measurements, recipes, maps, scale drawings, mixtures, and many other real-life situations.
