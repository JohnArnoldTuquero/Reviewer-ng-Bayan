What is Scientific Notation?

Scientific notation is a way of writing very large or very small numbers in a shorter and easier form. It is commonly used in mathematics, science, engineering, and other fields where extremely large or extremely small values are common.

For example, instead of writing 300,000,000, we can write:

3 × 108

Both expressions represent the same number. Scientific notation simply makes the number easier to read, write, and calculate with.

Examples

5,000 = 5 × 103

72,000,000 = 7.2 × 107

0.0004 = 4 × 10-4

The Standard Form

Scientific notation has the general form:

a × 10n

where a is a number greater than or equal to 1 but less than 10, and n is an integer.

Parts of Scientific Notation

In 4.5 × 106:

4.5 is called the coefficient.

10 is the base.

6 is the exponent.

The exponent tells us how many places the decimal point moves.

The coefficient must normally be between 1 and 10:

1 ≤ a < 10

Therefore, 45 × 103 is not in proper scientific notation. It should be written as:

4.5 × 104

Positive Exponents

A positive exponent is used when the original number is greater than or equal to 10.

When the exponent is positive, the decimal point moves to the right.

Example 1

3 × 104

Move the decimal point 4 places to the right:

3 → 30 → 300 → 3,000 → 30,000

Therefore:

3 × 104 = 30,000

Example 2

6.25 × 103

Move the decimal point 3 places to the right:

6.25 → 62.5 → 625 → 6,250

Therefore:

6.25 × 103 = 6,250

Negative Exponents

A negative exponent is used when the original number is between 0 and 1.

When the exponent is negative, the decimal point moves to the left.

Example 1

4 × 10-3

Move the decimal point 3 places to the left:

4 → 0.4 → 0.04 → 0.004

Therefore:

4 × 10-3 = 0.004

Example 2

7.5 × 10-4

Move the decimal point 4 places to the left:

7.5 → 0.75 → 0.075 → 0.0075 → 0.00075

Therefore:

7.5 × 10-4 = 0.00075

Converting Decimal Numbers to Scientific Notation

To convert an ordinary number into scientific notation, move the decimal point until the first part of the number is between 1 and 10.

For Large Numbers

Count how many places the decimal point moves to the left. The exponent will be positive.

Example

Convert 45,000 to scientific notation.

Move the decimal point 4 places to the left:

45,000 → 4.5

Therefore:

45,000 = 4.5 × 104

For Small Numbers

Count how many places the decimal point moves to the right. The exponent will be negative.

Example

Convert 0.00032 to scientific notation.

Move the decimal point 4 places to the right:

0.00032 → 3.2

Therefore:

0.00032 = 3.2 × 10-4

Important Rule

Remember:

Large number → positive exponent

Small number → negative exponent

Converting Scientific Notation to Decimal Form

To convert scientific notation back to ordinary decimal form, use the exponent to determine how many places to move the decimal point.

Positive Exponent

Move the decimal point to the right.

Example:

2.4 × 105

Move the decimal point 5 places to the right:

2.4 → 24 → 240 → 2,400 → 24,000 → 240,000

Therefore:

2.4 × 105 = 240,000

Negative Exponent

Move the decimal point to the left.

Example:

5.6 × 10-3

Move the decimal point 3 places to the left:

5.6 → 0.56 → 0.056 → 0.0056

Therefore:

5.6 × 10-3 = 0.0056

Multiplying Numbers in Scientific Notation

To multiply numbers in scientific notation:

1. Multiply the coefficients.

2. Add the exponents of 10.

3. Rewrite the answer in proper scientific notation if necessary.

Example

(3 × 104)(2 × 103)

Multiply the coefficients:

3 × 2 = 6

Add the exponents:

4 + 3 = 7

Therefore:

(3 × 104)(2 × 103) = 6 × 107

Another Example

(4 × 105)(3 × 102)

4 × 3 = 12

5 + 2 = 7

This gives:

12 × 107

Since 12 is greater than 10, rewrite it:

1.2 × 108

Dividing Numbers in Scientific Notation

To divide numbers in scientific notation:

1. Divide the coefficients.

2. Subtract the exponent of the denominator from the exponent of the numerator.

3. Rewrite the answer in proper scientific notation if necessary.

Example

(8 × 106) ÷ (2 × 102)

Divide the coefficients:

8 ÷ 2 = 4

Subtract the exponents:

6 − 2 = 4

Therefore:

(8 × 106) ÷ (2 × 102) = 4 × 104

Another Example

(6 × 103) ÷ (3 × 105)

6 ÷ 3 = 2

3 − 5 = -2

Therefore:

2 × 10-2

Adding and Subtracting Numbers in Scientific Notation

When adding or subtracting numbers in scientific notation, the powers of 10 must have the same exponent first.

After making the exponents the same, add or subtract the coefficients.

Example

3 × 105 + 2 × 105

Since both terms have the same exponent, simply add the coefficients:

3 + 2 = 5

Therefore:

3 × 105 + 2 × 105 = 5 × 105

Example with Different Exponents

4 × 105 + 3 × 104

Rewrite the second number using 105:

3 × 104 = 0.3 × 105

Now add:

4 × 105 + 0.3 × 105

4 + 0.3 = 4.3

Therefore:

4.3 × 105

Scientific Notation in Word Problems

Scientific notation is useful when solving problems involving extremely large or extremely small quantities.

Example 1: Distance

Suppose a distance is approximately 150,000,000 kilometers. Write this in scientific notation.

Move the decimal point 8 places to the left:

150,000,000 = 1.5 × 108

Example 2: Very Small Measurement

A measurement is 0.000006 meters. Write this in scientific notation.

Move the decimal point 6 places to the right:

0.000006 = 6 × 10-6

Problem-Solving Tip

When solving a word problem, first identify the numbers and their units. Convert them to scientific notation if appropriate, then perform the required operation.

Always check whether your final coefficient is between 1 and 10. If it is not, rewrite the answer into proper scientific notation.

Summary

Scientific notation is used to represent very large and very small numbers in a compact form.

The standard form is:

a × 10n

where 1 ≤ a < 10.

Important Rules

Positive exponent: Move the decimal point to the right.

Negative exponent: Move the decimal point to the left.

Multiplication: Multiply the coefficients and add the exponents.

Division: Divide the coefficients and subtract the exponents.

Addition and subtraction: Make the exponents equal before adding or subtracting the coefficients.

Quick Examples

50,000 = 5 × 104

0.0007 = 7 × 10-4

2 × 103 = 2,000

4 × 10-3 = 0.004

Scientific notation makes calculations with very large and very small numbers much easier and more organized.
