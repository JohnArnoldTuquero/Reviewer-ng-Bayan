What is Estimation?

Estimation is the process of finding a value that is close to an exact answer. Instead of calculating an exact result, we use numbers that are easier to work with.

Estimation is useful when an exact answer is not necessary or when we want to quickly determine whether an answer is reasonable.

Example

Suppose you want to estimate:

398 + 201

We can round the numbers:

398 ≈ 400

201 ≈ 200

Therefore:

398 + 201 ≈ 400 + 200 = 600

The exact answer is 599, while the estimated answer is 600. The estimate is very close to the exact answer.

What is Approximation?

Approximation means representing a number or measurement with a value that is close to the exact value.

The symbol ≈ means "approximately equal to."

Example

π is approximately:

π ≈ 3.14

The value of π continues indefinitely, so 3.14 is an approximation rather than its exact value.

Estimation vs. Approximation

Estimation usually involves finding a close answer quickly, often by rounding or using simpler numbers.

Approximation is a broader idea of representing an exact value with a nearby value.

Both are used when working with values where an exact result is unnecessary, difficult, or impossible to write conveniently.

Rounding Numbers

Rounding is one of the most common methods of estimation. It replaces a number with a nearby value that is easier to use.

The Basic Rounding Rule

Look at the digit immediately to the right of the place where you want to round.

If the digit is 5 or greater, increase the digit being rounded by 1.

If the digit is 4 or less, keep the digit being rounded unchanged.

Example

Round 47 to the nearest ten.

The ones digit is 7.

Since 7 is greater than 5, increase the tens digit:

47 ≈ 50

Another Example

Round 42 to the nearest ten.

The ones digit is 2.

Since 2 is less than 5:

42 ≈ 40

Rounding to the Nearest Ten

To round a whole number to the nearest ten, look at the ones digit.

If the ones digit is 5 or more, round up.

If the ones digit is 4 or less, round down.

Examples

34 → 30

35 → 40

67 → 70

82 → 80

96 → 100

Larger Number

Consider 4,327.

The ones digit is 7, so round the tens digit up:

4,327 ≈ 4,330

Rounding to the Nearest Hundred

To round to the nearest hundred, look at the tens digit.

If the tens digit is 5 or greater, round the hundreds digit up.

If the tens digit is 4 or less, keep the hundreds digit unchanged.

Examples

342 → 300

368 → 400

1,249 → 1,200

1,250 → 1,300

5,781 → 5,800

Example

Round 7,463 to the nearest hundred.

The tens digit is 6.

Since 6 is greater than 5, round up:

7,463 ≈ 7,500

Rounding to the Nearest Thousand

To round to the nearest thousand, look at the hundreds digit.

If the hundreds digit is 5 or greater, round up.

If the hundreds digit is 4 or less, round down.

Examples

3,241 → 3,000

3,651 → 4,000

8,499 → 8,000

8,500 → 9,000

Example

Round 27,634 to the nearest thousand.

The hundreds digit is 6.

Since 6 is greater than 5, round up:

27,634 ≈ 28,000

Rounding Decimals

Decimals can also be rounded to a specified place value such as the nearest whole number, tenth, hundredth, or thousandth.

Nearest Whole Number

Look at the tenths digit.

Example:

7.6 ≈ 8

7.3 ≈ 7

Nearest Tenth

Look at the hundredths digit.

Example:

4.26 ≈ 4.3

8.21 ≈ 8.2

Nearest Hundredth

Look at the thousandths digit.

Example:

3.146 ≈ 3.15

5.732 ≈ 5.73

Estimating Sums

To estimate a sum, round the numbers to convenient values and then add them.

Example

Estimate:

487 + 312

Round to the nearest hundred:

487 ≈ 500

312 ≈ 300

Add:

500 + 300 = 800

Therefore:

487 + 312 ≈ 800

The exact answer is 799, so the estimate is very close.

Estimating Differences

To estimate a difference, round the numbers before subtracting.

Example

Estimate:

1,982 − 641

Round to the nearest hundred:

1,982 ≈ 2,000

641 ≈ 600

Subtract:

2,000 − 600 = 1,400

Therefore:

1,982 − 641 ≈ 1,400

The exact answer is 1,341.

Estimating Products

A product is the result of multiplication. To estimate a product, round the factors to convenient numbers before multiplying.

Example

Estimate:

49 × 21

Round the factors:

49 ≈ 50

21 ≈ 20

Multiply:

50 × 20 = 1,000

Therefore:

49 × 21 ≈ 1,000

The exact answer is 1,029.

Estimating Quotients

A quotient is the result of division. To estimate a quotient, use numbers that are easy to divide.

Example

Estimate:

398 ÷ 8

Use a nearby compatible number:

400 ÷ 8 = 50

Therefore:

398 ÷ 8 ≈ 50

The exact quotient is 49.75, so 50 is a reasonable estimate.

Using Compatible Numbers

Compatible numbers are numbers that work well together in mental calculations. They are especially useful when estimating division.

Example

Estimate:

624 ÷ 8

624 is already divisible by 8:

624 ÷ 8 = 78

But consider:

617 ÷ 8

We can use 616 because it is close to 617 and is easily divisible by 8:

616 ÷ 8 = 77

Therefore:

617 ÷ 8 ≈ 77

Compatible numbers make estimation faster and easier.

Estimation in Word Problems

Estimation is useful in real-life situations where an exact answer may not be necessary.

Example: Shopping

You want to buy items costing ₱198, ₱302, and ₱97. About how much money will you need?

Round the prices:

₱198 ≈ ₱200

₱302 ≈ ₱300

₱97 ≈ ₱100

Add:

₱200 + ₱300 + ₱100 = ₱600

Therefore, you will need approximately ₱600.

Example: Distance

A person travels 48 km in the morning and 53 km in the afternoon. Estimate the total distance traveled.

48 ≈ 50

53 ≈ 50

50 + 50 = 100 km

The estimated total distance is approximately 100 km.

Checking for Reasonableness

Estimation can be used to determine whether an exact answer makes sense. This is called checking for reasonableness.

Example

Suppose someone calculates:

49 × 21 = 10,290

Is the answer reasonable?

Estimate:

49 ≈ 50

21 ≈ 20

50 × 20 = 1,000

The estimated answer is around 1,000, but 10,290 is much larger. Therefore, the calculated answer is not reasonable.

In fact:

49 × 21 = 1,029

Estimation is therefore a useful way to catch calculation errors.

Summary

Estimation is finding a value that is close to an exact answer. Approximation represents an exact value using a nearby value.

Important Rules

Rounding: Look at the digit immediately to the right of the place you are rounding.

5 or greater: Round up.

4 or less: Keep the digit unchanged.

Estimating sums: Round and add.

Estimating differences: Round and subtract.

Estimating products: Round and multiply.

Estimating quotients: Use convenient or compatible numbers and divide.

Estimation is especially useful for making quick calculations, solving real-life problems, and checking whether an answer is reasonable.

Quick Examples

398 + 201 ≈ 600

1,982 − 641 ≈ 1,400

49 × 21 ≈ 1,000

398 ÷ 8 ≈ 50

The key idea is to choose numbers that are close to the original values but easier to calculate with.
