Fractions

A fraction represents a part of a whole or a part of a group.

A fraction is written using two numbers:

Numerator / Denominator

For example:

3/4

The fraction 3/4 means that a whole has been divided into 4 equal parts and 3 of those parts are being considered.

Examples

1/2 means one out of two equal parts.

3/5 means three out of five equal parts.

7/10 means seven out of ten equal parts.

Parts of a Fraction

A fraction has two main parts: the numerator and the denominator.

Numerator

The numerator is the number on top of the fraction. It tells how many parts are being considered.

In 3/5, the numerator is 3.

Denominator

The denominator is the number at the bottom. It tells how many equal parts make up the whole.

In 3/5, the denominator is 5.

Important Rule

The denominator of a fraction can never be zero.

For example, 5/0 is undefined.

Proper and Improper Fractions

Proper Fractions

A proper fraction has a numerator that is smaller than its denominator.

Examples:

1/2, 3/5, 4/7

Proper fractions have a value less than 1.

Improper Fractions

An improper fraction has a numerator that is greater than or equal to its denominator.

Examples:

5/3, 7/4, 9/9

Improper fractions have a value greater than or equal to 1.

Example

7/4 = 1 3/4

Mixed Numbers

A mixed number contains a whole number and a proper fraction.

Example:

2 1/3

This means two whole units and one-third of another unit.

Converting a Mixed Number to an Improper Fraction

Multiply the whole number by the denominator, then add the numerator.

Example:

2 1/3

Multiply:

2 × 3 = 6

Add the numerator:

6 + 1 = 7

Keep the denominator:

2 1/3 = 7/3

Converting an Improper Fraction to a Mixed Number

Divide the numerator by the denominator.

Example:

11/4

11 ÷ 4 = 2 remainder 3

Therefore:

11/4 = 2 3/4

Equivalent Fractions

Equivalent fractions are fractions that have different numerators and denominators but represent the same value.

Example

1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6 = 4/8

All of these fractions represent the same amount.

Finding an Equivalent Fraction

Multiply both the numerator and denominator by the same nonzero number.

Example:

2/3

Multiply both parts by 4:

(2 × 4)/(3 × 4) = 8/12

Therefore:

2/3 = 8/12

Simplifying Fractions

Simplifying a fraction means reducing it to its simplest form.

To simplify a fraction, divide the numerator and denominator by their greatest common factor.

Example: 12/18

The greatest common factor of 12 and 18 is 6.

Divide both by 6:

12 ÷ 6 = 2

18 ÷ 6 = 3

Therefore:

12/18 = 2/3

Important

A fraction is in simplest form when the numerator and denominator have no common factor greater than 1.

Adding Fractions

Same Denominators

When fractions have the same denominator, add their numerators and keep the denominator.

Example:

2/7 + 3/7 = 5/7

Different Denominators

When fractions have different denominators, first find a common denominator.

Example:

1/2 + 1/3

The least common denominator is 6.

Convert the fractions:

1/2 = 3/6

1/3 = 2/6

Add:

3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6

Therefore:

1/2 + 1/3 = 5/6

Subtracting Fractions

Same Denominators

When fractions have the same denominator, subtract the numerators and keep the denominator.

Example:

5/8 - 2/8 = 3/8

Different Denominators

Find a common denominator before subtracting.

Example:

3/4 - 1/2

The common denominator is 4.

Convert:

1/2 = 2/4

Subtract:

3/4 - 2/4 = 1/4

Therefore:

3/4 - 1/2 = 1/4

Multiplying Fractions

To multiply fractions, multiply the numerators together and multiply the denominators together.

Example

2/3 × 4/5

Multiply the numerators:

2 × 4 = 8

Multiply the denominators:

3 × 5 = 15

Therefore:

2/3 × 4/5 = 8/15

Mixed Numbers

Convert mixed numbers to improper fractions before multiplying.

Example:

1 1/2 × 2/3

Convert:

1 1/2 = 3/2

Then:

3/2 × 2/3 = 6/6 = 1

Dividing Fractions

To divide fractions, multiply the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

The reciprocal is found by switching the numerator and denominator.

Example

2/3 ÷ 4/5

Change division to multiplication and find the reciprocal of 4/5:

2/3 × 5/4

Multiply:

10/12

Simplify:

10/12 = 5/6

Therefore:

2/3 ÷ 4/5 = 5/6

Remember

Keep → Change → Flip

Keep the first fraction, change division to multiplication, and flip the second fraction.

Decimals

A decimal is another way of representing a number that contains a whole-number part and/or a fractional part.

The decimal point separates the whole-number part from the fractional part.

Examples

5.25

0.75

12.5

Decimals can represent fractions.

For example:

0.5 = 1/2

0.25 = 1/4

0.75 = 3/4

Decimal Place Value

Each digit after the decimal point represents a fractional place value.

The places are:

Tenths → Hundredths → Thousandths → Ten-thousandths

Example: 4.582

In 4.582:

4 is in the ones place.

5 is in the tenths place.

8 is in the hundredths place.

2 is in the thousandths place.

Therefore:

4.582 = 4 + 5/10 + 8/100 + 2/1000

Important

The farther a digit is to the right of the decimal point, the smaller its place value becomes.

Converting Fractions to Decimals

To convert a fraction to a decimal, divide the numerator by the denominator.

Example: 1/2

Divide:

1 ÷ 2 = 0.5

Therefore:

1/2 = 0.5

Example: 3/4

3 ÷ 4 = 0.75

Therefore:

3/4 = 0.75

Example: 1/8

1 ÷ 8 = 0.125

Therefore:

1/8 = 0.125

Converting Decimals to Fractions

To convert a terminating decimal into a fraction, write the decimal as an integer over a power of 10, then simplify.

Example: 0.5

There is one digit after the decimal point, so use 10 as the denominator:

0.5 = 5/10

Simplify:

5/10 = 1/2

Therefore:

0.5 = 1/2

Example: 0.75

There are two digits after the decimal point, so use 100:

0.75 = 75/100

Divide the numerator and denominator by 25:

75/100 = 3/4

Therefore:

0.75 = 3/4

Example: 0.125

There are three digits after the decimal point:

0.125 = 125/1000

Simplify:

125/1000 = 1/8

Therefore:

0.125 = 1/8

Comparing Fractions and Decimals

Fractions and decimals can be compared by converting them into the same form.

Example

Compare:

1/2 and 0.6

Convert 1/2 to a decimal:

1/2 = 0.5

Now compare:

0.5 < 0.6

Therefore:

1/2 < 0.6

Example

Compare:

3/4 and 0.7

Convert:

3/4 = 0.75

Compare:

0.75 > 0.7

Therefore:

3/4 > 0.7

Summary

A fraction represents a part of a whole and consists of a numerator and denominator.

A proper fraction has a numerator smaller than its denominator.

An improper fraction has a numerator greater than or equal to its denominator.

A mixed number contains a whole number and a proper fraction.

Equivalent fractions have the same value even though their numerators and denominators may be different.

To simplify a fraction, divide the numerator and denominator by their greatest common factor.

To add or subtract fractions with different denominators, first find a common denominator.

To multiply fractions, multiply the numerators and denominators.

To divide fractions, multiply by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

A decimal uses a decimal point to separate the whole-number part from the fractional part.

To convert a fraction to a decimal, divide the numerator by the denominator.

To convert a terminating decimal to a fraction, write it over a power of 10 and simplify.

Fractions and decimals can be compared by converting them into the same form.
