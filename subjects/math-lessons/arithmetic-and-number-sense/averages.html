What is an Average?

An average is a value that represents a group of numbers. It gives us an idea of the typical or central value of a collection of numbers.

The most common type of average is the arithmetic mean. It is found by adding all the values and dividing the sum by the number of values.

Example

Consider the numbers:

10, 20, 30

Add the numbers:

10 + 20 + 30 = 60

There are 3 numbers.

60 ÷ 3 = 20

Therefore, the average is 20.

Arithmetic Mean

The arithmetic mean, commonly called the mean or average, is calculated by dividing the sum of all values by the number of values.

Formula

Mean = Sum of all values ÷ Number of values

Another way to write the formula is:

Mean = Σx ÷ n

where Σx represents the sum of all values and n represents the number of values.

Example

Find the mean of 8, 12, 15, and 25.

Sum:

8 + 12 + 15 + 25 = 60

Number of values = 4

Mean = 60 ÷ 4

Mean = 15

Finding the Mean

To find the mean of a set of numbers, follow these steps:

Step 1: Add all the values

Find the total or sum of all the numbers.

Step 2: Count the values

Determine how many numbers are in the set.

Step 3: Divide

Divide the sum by the number of values.

Example

Find the average of:

5, 10, 15, 20, 25

Sum:

5 + 10 + 15 + 20 + 25 = 75

Number of values = 5

Average:

75 ÷ 5 = 15

Therefore, the average is 15.

Finding a Missing Value

Sometimes the average and some of the values are given, but one value is missing. We can use the average formula to find the missing value.

Important Formula

Total = Average × Number of values

Example

The average of 5 numbers is 20. Four of the numbers are 12, 18, 25, and 15. Find the fifth number.

First, find the total of all 5 numbers:

Total = 20 × 5

Total = 100

Add the four known numbers:

12 + 18 + 25 + 15 = 70

Find the missing number:

100 − 70 = 30

Therefore, the missing number is 30.

Weighted Average

A weighted average is an average in which some values have more importance or influence than others.

This is commonly used when different activities, exams, or categories have different percentages or weights.

Formula

Weighted Average = Σ(Value × Weight) ÷ Σ(Weights)

Example

Suppose a student's grade is based on:

Quiz: 80 with a weight of 30%

Exam: 90 with a weight of 70%

Convert the percentages to decimals:

30% = 0.30

70% = 0.70

Multiply each score by its weight:

80 × 0.30 = 24

90 × 0.70 = 63

Add the weighted values:

24 + 63 = 87

Therefore, the weighted average is 87.

Average of Fractions

To find the average of fractions, add the fractions together and divide by the number of fractions.

Example

Find the average of:

1/2 and 3/4

First, add the fractions:

1/2 + 3/4

Convert 1/2 to fourths:

1/2 = 2/4

Therefore:

2/4 + 3/4 = 5/4

There are 2 fractions, so divide by 2:

5/4 ÷ 2 = 5/8

Therefore, the average is 5/8.

Average of Percentages

Percentages can also be averaged. When all percentages have equal importance, add them together and divide by the number of percentages.

Example

A student gets scores of 80%, 90%, and 70%. Find the average percentage.

Add the percentages:

80 + 90 + 70 = 240

Divide by 3:

240 ÷ 3 = 80

Therefore, the average is 80%.

Important

If the percentages have different weights, use a weighted average instead of a simple average.

Average Word Problems

Average problems often appear in situations involving test scores, ages, temperatures, salaries, distances, or other measurements.

Example 1: Test Scores

A student scored 75, 80, 85, and 90 on four tests. What is the average score?

Add the scores:

75 + 80 + 85 + 90 = 330

There are 4 scores.

330 ÷ 4 = 82.5

Therefore, the average score is 82.5.

Example 2: Ages

The ages of three people are 20, 25, and 30. What is their average age?

20 + 25 + 30 = 75

75 ÷ 3 = 25

Therefore, their average age is 25 years.

Combined Average

A combined average is the average of two or more groups considered together.

When groups have different numbers of values, simply averaging their averages may give an incorrect result. Instead, find the total of each group first.

Formula

Combined Average = (Total of Group 1 + Total of Group 2) ÷ (Number of values in Group 1 + Number of values in Group 2)

Example

Class A has 20 students with an average score of 80. Class B has 30 students with an average score of 90. Find the combined average.

Find the total score of Class A:

20 × 80 = 1,600

Find the total score of Class B:

30 × 90 = 2,700

Combined total:

1,600 + 2,700 = 4,300

Total number of students:

20 + 30 = 50

Combined average:

4,300 ÷ 50 = 86

Therefore, the combined average is 86.

Median

The median is the middle value in a set of numbers when the numbers are arranged from least to greatest.

Odd Number of Values

When there is an odd number of values, the median is the single middle number.

Example

Find the median of:

3, 7, 9, 12, 15

The numbers are already arranged from least to greatest.

The middle number is 9.

Therefore, the median is 9.

Even Number of Values

When there is an even number of values, find the average of the two middle numbers.

Example

Find the median of:

4, 6, 8, 10

The two middle numbers are 6 and 8.

Median = (6 + 8) ÷ 2

Median = 7

Therefore, the median is 7.

Mode

The mode is the value that appears most frequently in a set of numbers.

Example

Find the mode of:

2, 4, 4, 5, 6, 4, 8

The number 4 appears three times, which is more often than any other number.

Therefore, the mode is 4.

More Than One Mode

A set can have more than one mode if multiple values occur with the same highest frequency.

Example:

2, 2, 3, 3, 4, 5

Both 2 and 3 appear twice.

Therefore, the data set has two modes: 2 and 3.

Mean, Median, and Mode

Mean, median, and mode are all measures used to describe the center of a data set, but they are calculated differently.

Mean

Add all values and divide by the number of values.

Mean = Sum ÷ Number of values

Median

Arrange the values in order and find the middle value.

Mode

Find the value that occurs most frequently.

Example

Consider the data set:

2, 3, 3, 5, 7

Mean:

(2 + 3 + 3 + 5 + 7) ÷ 5 = 20 ÷ 5 = 4

Median:

The middle value is 3.

Mode:

The value that appears most often is 3.

Therefore:

Mean = 4

Median = 3

Mode = 3

Summary

An average is a value used to represent a group of numbers. The most common average is the arithmetic mean.

Important Formulas

Mean = Sum of values ÷ Number of values

Total = Mean × Number of values

Weighted Average = Σ(Value × Weight) ÷ Σ(Weights)

Measures of Central Tendency

Mean: Add all values and divide by the number of values.

Median: The middle value after arranging the data from least to greatest.

Mode: The value that appears most frequently.

Important Tips

Always count the number of values correctly before dividing.

When finding a median, arrange the numbers from least to greatest first.

When groups have different sizes, do not simply average their averages. Use the total values and total number of observations to find the combined average.

Averages are useful in many real-life situations, including test scores, grades, ages, measurements, salaries, distances, and other numerical data.
