What is Factoring?

Factoring is the process of rewriting an algebraic expression as a product of two or more simpler expressions called factors.

Factoring is essentially the reverse of multiplication.

For example:

3(x + 2) = 3x + 6

Multiplication changes 3(x + 2) into 3x + 6.

Factoring reverses the process:

3x + 6 = 3(x + 2)

Factoring is an important skill because it can simplify expressions and help solve polynomial equations.

Factors and Products

When two or more quantities are multiplied together, each quantity is called a factor, while the result is called the product.

For example:

4 × 6 = 24

The numbers 4 and 6 are factors, and 24 is the product.

The same idea applies to algebra.

(x + 3)(x + 2) = x2 + 5x + 6

The expressions (x + 3) and (x + 2) are factors of the polynomial x2 + 5x + 6.

Therefore, factoring a polynomial means finding expressions that can be multiplied together to produce the original polynomial.

Greatest Common Factor

The Greatest Common Factor (GCF) is the greatest factor that two or more terms have in common.

For example, consider:

12x2 and 18x

The numerical factors are:

12 → 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12

18 → 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18

The greatest common numerical factor is 6.

Both terms also contain x.

Therefore, the GCF is:

6x

Finding the GCF is often the first step when factoring a polynomial.

Factoring Out the GCF

To factor out the GCF, divide every term by the GCF and place the GCF outside parentheses.

Example

Factor:

12x2 + 18x

The GCF is 6x.

Divide each term by 6x:

12x2 ÷ 6x = 2x

18x ÷ 6x = 3

Therefore:

12x2 + 18x = 6x(2x + 3)

Always check that multiplying the GCF by the expression inside the parentheses gives the original polynomial.

Factoring Monomials

A monomial can be factored into simpler factors by separating its numerical and variable factors.

For example:

12x3

can be written as:

12 × x × x × x

or:

3 × 4 × x3

In polynomial factoring, the goal is usually to find useful factors that help simplify the entire expression.

For example:

8x2 + 12x

Both terms have a common factor of 4x:

8x2 + 12x = 4x(2x + 3)

Factoring by Grouping

Factoring by grouping is useful for polynomials with four or more terms. The terms are grouped into pairs, and a common factor is found in each group.

Example

Factor:

x3 + 3x2 + 2x + 6

Group the terms:

(x3 + 3x2) + (2x + 6)

Factor each group:

x2(x + 3) + 2(x + 3)

Both groups contain (x + 3).

Factor it out:

(x + 3)(x2 + 2)

Therefore:

x3 + 3x2 + 2x + 6 = (x + 3)(x2 + 2)

Factoring Trinomials

A trinomial is a polynomial with three terms.

A common quadratic trinomial has the form:

ax2 + bx + c

Factoring a trinomial means finding two binomials whose product gives the original trinomial.

For example:

x2 + 5x + 6

We need two numbers whose:

Product = 6

Sum = 5

The numbers are 2 and 3.

Therefore:

x2 + 5x + 6 = (x + 2)(x + 3)

Factoring x2 + bx + c

When the coefficient of x2 is 1, factoring can often be done by finding two numbers whose product is c and whose sum is b.

For:

x2 + bx + c

Find numbers m and n such that:

m × n = c

and:

m + n = b

Example

Factor:

x2 + 7x + 12

Find two numbers that multiply to 12 and add to 7.

3 × 4 = 12

3 + 4 = 7

Therefore:

x2 + 7x + 12 = (x + 3)(x + 4)

Example with Negative Terms

Factor:

x2 - 5x + 6

We need two numbers that multiply to 6 and add to -5.

Those numbers are -2 and -3.

Therefore:

x2 - 5x + 6 = (x - 2)(x - 3)

Factoring ax2 + bx + c

When the coefficient of x2 is greater than 1, factoring requires additional steps.

Consider:

6x2 + 11x + 3

Multiply the first coefficient and the constant:

6 × 3 = 18

Find two numbers whose product is 18 and whose sum is 11.

9 and 2

Rewrite the middle term:

6x2 + 9x + 2x + 3

Group the terms:

(6x2 + 9x) + (2x + 3)

Factor each group:

3x(2x + 3) + 1(2x + 3)

Factor out the common binomial:

(3x + 1)(2x + 3)

Therefore:

6x2 + 11x + 3 = (3x + 1)(2x + 3)

Perfect Square Trinomials

A perfect square trinomial is a trinomial that can be written as the square of a binomial.

The two important patterns are:

a2 + 2ab + b2 = (a + b)2

a2 - 2ab + b2 = (a - b)2

Example

Factor:

x2 + 8x + 16

Recognize:

x2 = x2

16 = 42

The middle term is:

2(x)(4) = 8x

Therefore:

x2 + 8x + 16 = (x + 4)2

These expressions can also be recognized by checking whether the first and last terms are perfect squares and whether the middle term is twice their product.

Difference of Two Squares

The difference of two squares is an expression in the form:

a2 - b2

Its factoring pattern is:

a2 - b2 = (a + b)(a - b)

Example

Factor:

9x2 - 25

Rewrite each term as a square:

(3x)2 - 52

Apply the formula:

(3x + 5)(3x - 5)

Therefore:

9x2 - 25 = (3x + 5)(3x - 5)

Remember that a sum of two squares, such as x2 + 25, does not factor over the real numbers using this difference-of-squares pattern.

Sum and Difference of Cubes

Expressions involving cubes can also be factored using special formulas.

Sum of Two Cubes

a3 + b3 = (a + b)(a2 - ab + b2)

Example:

x3 + 8

Since 8 = 23:

x3 + 23

Therefore:

(x + 2)(x2 - 2x + 4)

Difference of Two Cubes

a3 - b3 = (a - b)(a2 + ab + b2)

Example:

x3 - 27

Since 27 = 33:

x3 - 33

Therefore:

(x - 3)(x2 + 3x + 9)

Factoring Completely

Sometimes a polynomial can be factored more than once. Factoring completely means continuing the factoring process until no factor can be factored further using the method being considered.

Example

Factor completely:

2x2 + 8x + 6

First, find the GCF.

The GCF is 2.

Factor it out:

2(x2 + 4x + 3)

Now factor the trinomial:

x2 + 4x + 3 = (x + 1)(x + 3)

Therefore, the complete factorization is:

2(x + 1)(x + 3)

It is important to check for a GCF before applying other factoring techniques.

Checking a Factored Expression

A factored expression can be checked by multiplying the factors back together.

Suppose we have:

(x + 2)(x + 5)

Multiply using the distributive property:

x(x) + x(5) + 2(x) + 2(5)

Simplify:

x2 + 5x + 2x + 10

Combine like terms:

x2 + 7x + 10

Therefore:

(x + 2)(x + 5) = x2 + 7x + 10

If the expanded expression matches the original polynomial, the factoring is correct.

Common Mistakes

Factoring requires careful attention to signs, coefficients, and common factors.

1. Forgetting the GCF

Always check whether all terms have a common factor before using another factoring method.

2. Incorrect Signs

For example:

x2 - 5x + 6

must be factored as:

(x - 2)(x - 3)

because -2 + -3 = -5 and (-2)(-3) = 6.

3. Forgetting to Factor Completely

After factoring once, check whether the resulting factors can be factored further.

4. Confusing Difference of Squares with Sum of Squares

The formula:

a2 - b2 = (a + b)(a - b)

requires subtraction between the squares.

5. Not Checking the Answer

Multiply the factors back together to verify that they produce the original polynomial.

Factoring in Word Problems

Factoring can be useful in problems involving area, dimensions, and other quantities represented by algebraic expressions.

Example

Suppose the area of a rectangle is represented by:

x2 + 7x + 12

To determine possible expressions for its length and width, factor the polynomial.

Find two numbers whose product is 12 and whose sum is 7.

The numbers are 3 and 4.

Therefore:

x2 + 7x + 12 = (x + 3)(x + 4)

This means the dimensions can be represented by:

Length = x + 4

Width = x + 3

Factoring allows a polynomial area expression to be rewritten as the product of expressions representing the dimensions.

Summary

Factoring is the process of rewriting a polynomial as a product of simpler factors.

Important factoring methods include:

• Greatest Common Factor – factor out the largest factor shared by all terms.

• Factoring by Grouping – group terms and factor each group.

• Factoring Trinomials – find factors whose product and sum match the required coefficients.

• Perfect Square Trinomials – recognize expressions that are squares of binomials.

• Difference of Two Squares – use a2 - b2 = (a + b)(a - b).

• Sum of Two Cubes – use a3 + b3 = (a + b)(a2 - ab + b2).

• Difference of Two Cubes – use a3 - b3 = (a - b)(a2 + ab + b2).

When factoring a polynomial, use this general approach:

1. Look for a GCF.

2. Identify the type of polynomial.

3. Look for a special product pattern.

4. Factor the remaining expression.

5. Factor completely.

6. Multiply the factors back together to check your answer.

Factoring is an essential algebra skill because it is used in simplifying expressions, solving polynomial equations, and working with quadratic functions.
