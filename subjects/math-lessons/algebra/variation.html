What is Variation?

Variation describes a relationship between two or more quantities where a change in one quantity is related to a change in another quantity.

In mathematics, variation is commonly used to describe how one variable changes in relation to another variable.

There are several types of variation:

  • Direct Variation – one quantity increases or decreases in the same proportion as another.
  • Inverse Variation – one quantity increases while another decreases in such a way that their product remains constant.
  • Joint Variation – one quantity varies directly with the product of two or more other quantities.
  • Combined Variation – a relationship involving both direct and inverse variation.

Variation is useful for modeling real-life relationships such as speed and time, cost and quantity, and work and the number of workers.

Constant of Variation

The constant of variation is a fixed number that describes the relationship between variables.

It is usually represented by the letter k.

For direct variation:

y = kx

The constant can be found using:

k = y/x

For inverse variation:

y = k/x

The constant can be found using:

k = xy

The value of k remains constant for the same variation relationship.

Direct Variation

Direct variation occurs when one quantity changes directly in proportion to another quantity.

If y varies directly as x, then:

y ∝ x

This means that when x increases, y also increases. When x decreases, y also decreases.

For example, if one notebook costs ₱20, then the total cost varies directly with the number of notebooks.

1 notebook → ₱20

2 notebooks → ₱40

3 notebooks → ₱60

The cost increases in the same proportion as the number of notebooks.

Direct Variation Formula

The formula for direct variation is:

y = kx

where:

  • y = dependent variable
  • x = independent variable
  • k = constant of variation

To find the constant of variation:

k = y/x

Therefore, if you know the values of x and y, you can find k and use it to find another value.

Direct Variation Examples

Suppose y varies directly as x, and when x = 4, y = 20. Find y when x = 7.

First find the constant:

k = y/x

k = 20/4 = 5

Therefore:

y = 5x

When x = 7:

y = 5(7)

y = 35

Therefore, when x = 7, y = 35.

Inverse Variation

Inverse variation occurs when one quantity increases while another quantity decreases in such a way that their product remains constant.

If y varies inversely as x, then:

y ∝ 1/x

For example, if a fixed amount of work must be completed, increasing the number of workers can decrease the amount of time needed.

In an inverse relationship, the product of the two variables remains constant.

xy = k

Inverse Variation Formula

The formula for inverse variation is:

y = k/x

Another form is:

xy = k

where k is the constant of variation.

To find the constant:

k = xy

Unlike direct variation, the variables move in opposite directions. When one increases, the other generally decreases.

Inverse Variation Examples

Suppose y varies inversely as x. When x = 5, y = 12. Find y when x = 10.

First find the constant:

k = xy

k = 5(12) = 60

Now use:

y = 60/x

When x = 10:

y = 60/10

y = 6

Therefore, when x = 10, y = 6.

Joint Variation

Joint variation occurs when one variable varies directly with the product of two or more other variables.

For example, if z varies jointly as x and y, then:

z ∝ xy

This means that z depends directly on both x and y.

If either x or y increases while the other remains constant, z also increases.

Joint Variation Formula

The formula for joint variation is:

z = kxy

where:

  • z = dependent variable
  • x and y = independent variables
  • k = constant of variation

The constant can be found using:

k = z/(xy)

Example

If z = 24 when x = 2 and y = 4, then:

24 = k(2)(4)

24 = 8k

k = 3

Therefore, the variation equation is:

z = 3xy

Combined Variation

Combined variation involves both direct and inverse variation in the same relationship.

For example, suppose z varies directly as x and inversely as y.

This means:

z ∝ x/y

The corresponding equation is:

z = kx/y

Here, z increases when x increases, but decreases when y increases, assuming the other variable remains constant.

Combined Variation Formula

A common form of combined variation is:

z = kx/y

To find the constant:

k = zy/x

Example

Suppose z varies directly as x and inversely as y. If x = 6, y = 3, and z = 8, find the constant of variation.

Use:

z = kx/y

Substitute:

8 = k(6)/3

8 = 2k

k = 4

Therefore, the constant of variation is 4.

Finding the Constant of Variation

The first step in most variation problems is finding the constant k.

Direct Variation

k = y/x

Inverse Variation

k = xy

Joint Variation

k = z/(xy)

Combined Variation

For z = kx/y:

k = zy/x

Once k is known, substitute it into the variation equation and use the equation to find the unknown value.

Finding an Unknown Value

After finding the constant of variation, substitute it into the appropriate equation.

Example

Suppose y varies directly as x. If y = 18 when x = 6, find y when x = 15.

Find k:

k = 18/6 = 3

Therefore:

y = 3x

When x = 15:

y = 3(15)

y = 45

Therefore, the unknown value is 45.

Variation Word Problems

Variation is commonly used in real-life situations. The key is to identify the type of relationship described in the problem.

Example 1: Direct Variation

The cost of several identical notebooks varies directly with the number of notebooks purchased. If 4 notebooks cost ₱80, how much will 7 notebooks cost?

Since cost varies directly with quantity:

C = kx

Find the constant:

k = 80/4 = 20

For 7 notebooks:

C = 20(7) = 140

Therefore, 7 notebooks cost ₱140.

Example 2: Inverse Variation

A task takes 12 hours when 4 workers are assigned to it. Assuming the amount of work remains fixed, how long would it take 6 workers?

The number of workers and the time vary inversely:

wt = k

Find the constant:

k = 4(12) = 48

For 6 workers:

6t = 48

t = 8

Therefore, under the stated assumption, the task would take 8 hours.

Common Mistakes

When solving variation problems, avoid these common mistakes:

  • Confusing direct variation with inverse variation.
  • Using k = y/x for an inverse variation.
  • Forgetting to find the constant of variation first.
  • Using the wrong variable in the variation equation.
  • Forgetting that inverse variation involves a product that remains constant.
  • Failing to identify whether a problem involves direct, inverse, joint, or combined variation.
  • Substituting values into the wrong formula.
  • Not checking whether the final answer makes sense in the given situation.

A useful strategy is to first translate the words into a mathematical relationship before solving.

Summary

Variation describes how quantities change in relation to one another.

Direct Variation:

y = kx

Inverse Variation:

y = k/x

Joint Variation:

z = kxy

Combined Variation:

z = kx/y

Remember:

  • Direct → variables change in the same direction.
  • Inverse → one increases while the other decreases, with their product remaining constant.
  • Joint → one variable varies directly with the product of two or more variables.
  • Combined → includes both direct and inverse relationships.
  • k → constant of variation.
