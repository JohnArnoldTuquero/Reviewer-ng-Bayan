What are Algebraic Word Problems?

Algebraic word problems are real-life situations that can be translated into mathematical expressions or equations and solved using algebra.

Instead of being given an equation directly, you are given information in words. Your job is to identify the unknown quantity, represent it with a variable, translate the information into an equation, and solve for the unknown.

For example:

A number increased by 8 is 20. What is the number?

Let the unknown number be x.

x + 8 = 20

Solving:

x = 12

Therefore, the number is 12.

Steps in Solving Algebraic Word Problems

A systematic approach makes word problems easier to solve.

Step 1: Read the Problem Carefully

Read the entire problem and determine what information is given and what needs to be found.

Step 2: Identify the Unknown

Choose a variable, usually x, to represent the unknown quantity.

Step 3: Translate the Information

Convert the words into mathematical expressions or equations.

Step 4: Write an Equation

Use the information in the problem to create an equation.

Step 5: Solve the Equation

Use algebraic operations to find the value of the variable.

Step 6: Check the Answer

Substitute your answer back into the original situation to make sure it satisfies the conditions of the problem.

Step 7: State the Answer

Give the answer using the appropriate unit or description.

Identifying the Unknown

The first important step is deciding what the variable represents.

For example:

A notebook costs ₱15 more than a pen. If the notebook costs ₱35, how much does the pen cost?

The unknown is the price of the pen.

Let:

x = price of the pen

Since the notebook costs ₱15 more:

x + 15 = 35

Then:

x = 20

Therefore, the pen costs ₱20.

Translating Words into Algebra

Knowing common mathematical phrases helps you translate word problems into equations.

Addition

"A number plus 7" can be written as:

x + 7

Subtraction

"A number minus 5" can be written as:

x - 5

Multiplication

"Three times a number" can be written as:

3x

Division

"A number divided by 4" can be written as:

x/4

Equality

"A number is 15" means:

x = 15

Some common phrases include:

  • sum → addition
  • difference → subtraction
  • product → multiplication
  • quotient → division
  • more than → addition
  • less than → subtraction
  • times → multiplication
  • is → equals

Addition Word Problems

Addition word problems involve combining quantities or increasing a quantity.

Example

A number plus 14 is equal to 39. Find the number.

Let the number be x.

x + 14 = 39

Subtract 14 from both sides:

x = 25

Therefore, the number is 25.

Another Example

Maria has 18 books. She receives some more books and now has 31 books. How many books did she receive?

Let x be the number of books received.

18 + x = 31

x = 13

Maria received 13 books.

Subtraction Word Problems

Subtraction problems often involve finding a difference, removing an amount, or comparing two quantities.

Example

A number decreased by 9 is 26. Find the number.

Let the number be x.

x - 9 = 26

Add 9 to both sides:

x = 35

Therefore, the number is 35.

Example with Comparison

John has 12 more points than Mark. John has 40 points. How many points does Mark have?

Let x be Mark's points.

x + 12 = 40

x = 28

Mark has 28 points.

Multiplication Word Problems

Multiplication word problems often involve equal groups, repeated quantities, or a quantity that is a certain number of times another quantity.

Example

Three times a number is 42. Find the number.

Let the number be x.

3x = 42

Divide both sides by 3:

x = 14

Therefore, the number is 14.

Example with Cost

Five identical notebooks cost ₱150. How much does one notebook cost?

Let x be the price of one notebook.

5x = 150

x = 30

Each notebook costs ₱30.

Division Word Problems

Division word problems involve sharing, grouping, or finding a quantity when a total is divided into equal parts.

Example

A number divided by 6 is 8. Find the number.

Let the number be x.

x/6 = 8

Multiply both sides by 6:

x = 48

Therefore, the number is 48.

Example with Sharing

A total of 96 candies are divided equally among 8 students. How many candies does each student receive?

Let x be the number of candies each student receives.

8x = 96

x = 12

Each student receives 12 candies.

Consecutive Integer Problems

Consecutive integers are integers that follow one another.

Examples:

4, 5, 6, 7

If the first integer is x, consecutive integers can be represented as:

x
x + 1
x + 2
x + 3

Example

The sum of three consecutive integers is 36. Find the integers.

Let the first integer be x.

The three integers are:

x, x + 1, x + 2

Write the equation:

x + (x + 1) + (x + 2) = 36

Combine like terms:

3x + 3 = 36

3x = 33

x = 11

The integers are:

11, 12, 13

Age Problems

Age problems use variables to represent unknown ages and equations to describe relationships between ages.

Example

Maria is 4 years older than Ana. Their ages add up to 30. Find their ages.

Let Ana's age be x.

Maria's age is:

x + 4

Their total age is 30:

x + (x + 4) = 30

Simplify:

2x + 4 = 30

2x = 26

x = 13

Ana is 13 years old.

Maria is:

13 + 4 = 17

Therefore, their ages are 13 and 17.

Number Problems

Number problems describe relationships between unknown numbers.

Example

The sum of a number and twice the number is 45. Find the number.

Let the number be x.

Twice the number is 2x.

The equation is:

x + 2x = 45

Combine like terms:

3x = 45

x = 15

Therefore, the number is 15.

Money Problems

Money problems often involve prices, quantities, totals, discounts, or combinations of different items.

Example

A student buys a pen for ₱20 and several notebooks costing ₱30 each. The total cost is ₱170. How many notebooks were purchased?

Let x be the number of notebooks.

The cost of the notebooks is:

30x

The total cost is:

20 + 30x = 170

Subtract 20:

30x = 150

Divide by 30:

x = 5

Therefore, the student purchased 5 notebooks.

Distance, Rate, and Time Problems

Problems involving motion can be solved using the relationship:

d = rt

where:

  • d = distance
  • r = rate or speed
  • t = time

Example

A car travels at 60 kilometers per hour for 3 hours. How far does it travel?

Use:

d = rt

Substitute:

d = 60(3)

d = 180

The car travels 180 kilometers.

Finding Time

If the distance is 240 kilometers and the speed is 60 kilometers per hour:

240 = 60t

t = 4

The travel time is 4 hours.

Work Problems

Work problems involve people or machines completing a task. The total amount of work can often be represented by the product of a rate and time.

Example

A worker can complete a task in 6 hours at a constant rate. What fraction of the task can the worker complete in 2 hours?

The work rate is:

1/6 of the task per hour.

In 2 hours:

2(1/6) = 2/6 = 1/3

Therefore, the worker can complete 1/3 of the task in 2 hours.

For problems involving multiple workers, make sure the rates are expressed using the same unit of time.

Geometry Word Problems

Algebra can be used to find unknown lengths, widths, areas, and perimeters in geometry problems.

Example

A rectangle has a length that is 5 cm longer than its width. Its perimeter is 34 cm. Find its dimensions.

Let the width be x.

The length is:

x + 5

The perimeter of a rectangle is:

P = 2l + 2w

Substitute the given information:

34 = 2(x + 5) + 2x

Expand:

34 = 2x + 10 + 2x

34 = 4x + 10

24 = 4x

x = 6

The width is 6 cm.

The length is:

6 + 5 = 11 cm

Therefore, the dimensions are 6 cm by 11 cm.

Percent Word Problems

Algebra can also be used to solve problems involving percentages.

Example

A shirt is discounted by 20%. The sale price is ₱640. What was the original price?

Let the original price be x.

A 20% discount means that 80% of the original price remains.

Therefore:

0.80x = 640

Divide by 0.80:

x = 800

The original price was ₱800.

Mixture Problems

Mixture problems involve combining two or more quantities with different prices, concentrations, or properties.

Example

A store combines coffee worth ₱200 per kilogram with coffee worth ₱300 per kilogram. If 2 kilograms of the first coffee and 3 kilograms of the second coffee are mixed, what is the total value of the mixture?

Value of the first coffee:

2(200) = 400

Value of the second coffee:

3(300) = 900

Total value:

400 + 900 = 1,300

Total amount of coffee:

2 + 3 = 5 kg

Average price per kilogram:

1300/5 = 260

Therefore, the mixture is worth ₱260 per kilogram.

Checking the Answer

Always check your answer after solving a word problem. A correct algebraic solution should also make sense in the original situation.

Example

Suppose you solve a problem and obtain:

x = 12

If the original equation was:

3x + 4 = 40

Substitute 12:

3(12) + 4 = 40

36 + 4 = 40

40 = 40

The equation is true, so the solution satisfies the equation.

Also check whether the answer is reasonable. For example, a person's age, number of objects, or amount of money should normally fit the conditions described in the problem.

Common Mistakes

When solving algebraic word problems, avoid these common mistakes:

  • Choosing the wrong variable for the unknown.
  • Writing an equation before fully understanding the problem.
  • Translating phrases incorrectly.
  • Confusing "more than" with "less than."
  • Forgetting units such as pesos, meters, hours, or kilometers.
  • Using the wrong formula for the situation.
  • Solving the equation incorrectly after setting it up correctly.
  • Failing to check the answer in the original problem.
  • Giving only the numerical answer without explaining what the variable represents.

A useful habit is to write a sentence defining the variable before creating the equation.

Summary

Algebraic word problems translate real-life situations into mathematical equations.

A reliable process is:

  1. Read the problem carefully.
  2. Identify what is unknown.
  3. Define a variable.
  4. Translate the information into algebra.
  5. Write an equation.
  6. Solve the equation.
  7. Check the solution.
  8. State the answer with the appropriate units.

Common types of algebraic word problems include:

  • Number problems
  • Consecutive integer problems
  • Age problems
  • Money problems
  • Distance, rate, and time problems
  • Work problems
  • Geometry problems
  • Percent problems
  • Mixture problems

The most important skill is learning how to translate the words of the problem into a mathematical relationship.
