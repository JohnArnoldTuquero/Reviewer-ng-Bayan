Order of Operations

The order of operations is a set of rules used to determine the correct sequence for performing mathematical operations in an expression.

When a mathematical expression contains several operations such as addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, parentheses, and exponents, the operations must be performed in a specific order.

Following the order of operations ensures that a mathematical expression has one consistent answer.


Why Is the Order of Operations Important?

Consider the expression:

2 + 3 × 4

If we simply calculate from left to right, we might first add 2 and 3:

2 + 3 = 5

Then multiply:

5 × 4 = 20

However, multiplication must be performed before addition.

First:

3 × 4 = 12

Then:

2 + 12 = 14

Therefore:

2 + 3 × 4 = 14


The Basic Order

The basic order of operations is:

1. Parentheses or other grouping symbols

2. Exponents

3. Multiplication and Division

4. Addition and Subtraction

This order can be remembered using the acronym PEMDAS.


Important Reminder

PEMDAS does not mean that multiplication must always be done before division, or that addition must always be done before subtraction.

Multiplication and division have equal priority and are performed from left to right.

Addition and subtraction have equal priority and are also performed from left to right.


PEMDAS

PEMDAS is a mnemonic that helps us remember the order in which mathematical operations should be performed.

The letters stand for:

P – Parentheses

E – Exponents

M – Multiplication

D – Division

A – Addition

S – Subtraction


PEMDAS in Order

The complete sequence is:

Parentheses → Exponents → Multiplication/Division → Addition/Subtraction


Example

Solve:

8 + 2 × 5

There are no parentheses or exponents.

Next, perform multiplication:

2 × 5 = 10

Then perform addition:

8 + 10 = 18

Therefore:

8 + 2 × 5 = 18


PEMDAS Does Not Mean Strictly Left-to-Right for Every Operation

Operations at the same level of priority are performed from left to right.

For example:

24 ÷ 6 × 2

Division and multiplication have equal priority.

Start from the left:

24 ÷ 6 = 4

Then:

4 × 2 = 8

Therefore:

24 ÷ 6 × 2 = 8


The same rule applies to addition and subtraction.

For example:

15 - 6 + 4

15 - 6 = 9

9 + 4 = 13

Therefore:

15 - 6 + 4 = 13


Parentheses and Grouping Symbols

Parentheses and other grouping symbols indicate which operations should be performed first.

Common grouping symbols include:

( ) Parentheses

[ ] Brackets

{ } Braces

When grouping symbols are present, solve the expression inside them before performing operations outside them.


Example 1

Solve:

(5 + 3) × 2

First, solve inside the parentheses:

5 + 3 = 8

Then multiply:

8 × 2 = 16

Therefore:

(5 + 3) × 2 = 16


Example 2

Solve:

20 - (6 + 4)

First:

6 + 4 = 10

Then:

20 - 10 = 10

Therefore:

20 - (6 + 4) = 10


Nested Grouping Symbols

Sometimes one grouping symbol appears inside another grouping symbol.

In this situation, begin with the innermost grouping symbol and work outward.

For example:

2 × [3 + (4 + 1)]

First solve the innermost parentheses:

4 + 1 = 5

Now:

2 × [3 + 5]

Next solve the brackets:

3 + 5 = 8

Finally:

2 × 8 = 16

Therefore:

2 × [3 + (4 + 1)] = 16


Quick Rule

Always solve the innermost grouping symbols first.


Exponents

An exponent indicates how many times a number, called the base, is multiplied by itself.

For example:

2³ = 2 × 2 × 2 = 8

The number 2 is the base, while 3 is the exponent.


Exponents in PEMDAS

After solving parentheses and other grouping symbols, solve the exponents before multiplication, division, addition, or subtraction.


Example 1

Solve:

3 + 2²

First solve the exponent:

2² = 4

Then add:

3 + 4 = 7

Therefore:

3 + 2² = 7


Example 2

Solve:

5 × 2³

First:

2³ = 8

Then:

5 × 8 = 40

Therefore:

5 × 2³ = 40


Example 3

Solve:

(3 + 1)²

First solve the parentheses:

3 + 1 = 4

Then solve the exponent:

4² = 16

Therefore:

(3 + 1)² = 16


Important Note

The exponent applies to the base immediately associated with it, including a grouped quantity when parentheses are used.

For example:

3² = 9

and:

(3 + 2)² = 25


Multiplication and Division

After parentheses and exponents have been evaluated, multiplication and division are performed.

Multiplication and division have the same priority.

Therefore, when both appear in the same expression, solve them from left to right.


Example 1

Solve:

8 + 4 × 3

First multiply:

4 × 3 = 12

Then add:

8 + 12 = 20

Therefore:

8 + 4 × 3 = 20


Example 2

Solve:

24 ÷ 6 × 2

Division and multiplication have equal priority, so work from left to right.

24 ÷ 6 = 4

4 × 2 = 8

Therefore:

24 ÷ 6 × 2 = 8


Example 3

Solve:

30 - 12 ÷ 3

First divide:

12 ÷ 3 = 4

Then subtract:

30 - 4 = 26

Therefore:

30 - 12 ÷ 3 = 26


Example 4

Solve:

40 ÷ 5 × 3

Work from left to right:

40 ÷ 5 = 8

8 × 3 = 24

Therefore:

40 ÷ 5 × 3 = 24


Quick Rule

Multiplication and Division → Solve from left to right.


Addition and Subtraction

Addition and subtraction are performed after parentheses, exponents, multiplication, and division.

Addition and subtraction have the same priority.

Therefore, when both appear in an expression, solve them from left to right.


Example 1

Solve:

10 - 4 + 3

Start from the left:

10 - 4 = 6

Then:

6 + 3 = 9

Therefore:

10 - 4 + 3 = 9


Example 2

Solve:

18 + 5 - 7

18 + 5 = 23

23 - 7 = 16

Therefore:

18 + 5 - 7 = 16


Example 3

Solve:

20 - 8 + 5 - 3

Work from left to right:

20 - 8 = 12

12 + 5 = 17

17 - 3 = 14

Therefore:

20 - 8 + 5 - 3 = 14


Quick Rule

Addition and Subtraction → Solve from left to right.


Solving Expressions Step-by-Step

When solving an expression with several operations, it is helpful to work through the expression one step at a time.

The general process is:

Step 1: Solve parentheses and other grouping symbols.

Step 2: Solve exponents.

Step 3: Solve multiplication and division from left to right.

Step 4: Solve addition and subtraction from left to right.


Example 1

Solve:

5 + 3 × 2

Step 1: There are no parentheses.

Step 2: There are no exponents.

Step 3: Perform multiplication:

3 × 2 = 6

Now:

5 + 6

Step 4: Add:

5 + 6 = 11

Therefore:

5 + 3 × 2 = 11


Example 2

Solve:

(8 - 3) × 2²

Step 1: Parentheses

8 - 3 = 5

Now:

5 × 2²

Step 2: Exponents

2² = 4

Now:

5 × 4

Step 3: Multiplication

5 × 4 = 20

Therefore:

(8 - 3) × 2² = 20


Example 3

Solve:

18 ÷ (3 + 3) + 2 × 4

Step 1: Parentheses

3 + 3 = 6

Now:

18 ÷ 6 + 2 × 4

Step 2: Multiplication and Division

18 ÷ 6 = 3

2 × 4 = 8

Now:

3 + 8

Step 3: Addition

3 + 8 = 11

Therefore:

18 ÷ (3 + 3) + 2 × 4 = 11


Example 4

Solve:

20 - 2 × (3 + 4) + 6

Step 1: Parentheses

3 + 4 = 7

Now:

20 - 2 × 7 + 6

Step 2: Multiplication

2 × 7 = 14

Now:

20 - 14 + 6

Step 3: Addition and Subtraction from left to right

20 - 14 = 6

6 + 6 = 12

Therefore:

20 - 2 × (3 + 4) + 6 = 12


Common Mistakes

Understanding common mistakes can help prevent errors when applying the order of operations.


Mistake 1: Solving Everything from Left to Right

A common mistake is to solve every operation from left to right without following PEMDAS.

For example:

4 + 5 × 2

Incorrect approach:

4 + 5 = 9

9 × 2 = 18

Correct approach:

5 × 2 = 10

4 + 10 = 14

Therefore:

4 + 5 × 2 = 14


Mistake 2: Always Doing Multiplication Before Division

Multiplication does not automatically come before division.

They have equal priority and should be solved from left to right.

For example:

16 ÷ 4 × 2

16 ÷ 4 = 4

4 × 2 = 8

Answer: 8


Mistake 3: Always Doing Addition Before Subtraction

Addition does not automatically come before subtraction.

They have equal priority and should be solved from left to right.

For example:

15 - 5 + 2

15 - 5 = 10

10 + 2 = 12

Answer: 12


Mistake 4: Ignoring Parentheses

Expressions inside parentheses must be handled before operations outside the parentheses.

For example:

6 × (2 + 3)

First:

2 + 3 = 5

Then:

6 × 5 = 30

Answer: 30


Mistake 5: Forgetting Exponents

Exponents must be evaluated before multiplication, division, addition, and subtraction.

For example:

2 + 3²

First:

3² = 9

Then:

2 + 9 = 11

Answer: 11


Summary

The order of operations is used to determine the correct sequence for solving mathematical expressions.

The acronym PEMDAS helps remember the order:

P – Parentheses

E – Exponents

M – Multiplication

D – Division

A – Addition

S – Subtraction


Important Rules to Remember

1. Solve parentheses and other grouping symbols first.

2. Solve exponents next.

3. Perform multiplication and division from left to right.

4. Perform addition and subtraction from left to right.

5. Multiplication and division have equal priority.

6. Addition and subtraction have equal priority.


Quick Review

Parentheses → Exponents → Multiplication/Division → Addition/Subtraction


Final Example

Solve:

10 + 2 × (6 - 2)² ÷ 8

Step 1: Parentheses

6 - 2 = 4

Now:

10 + 2 × 4² ÷ 8

Step 2: Exponent

4² = 16

Now:

10 + 2 × 16 ÷ 8

Step 3: Multiplication and Division from left to right

2 × 16 = 32

32 ÷ 8 = 4

Now:

10 + 4

Step 4: Addition

10 + 4 = 14

Therefore:

10 + 2 × (6 - 2)² ÷ 8 = 14


Always follow PEMDAS carefully and remember that operations with equal priority are solved from left to right.
